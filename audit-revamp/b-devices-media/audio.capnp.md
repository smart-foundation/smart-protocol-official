# audit: `schema/audio.capnp` (1263 lines)

## Verdict
**FIX (targeted) — the highest-value schema in this batch.** audio.capnp is *doctrinally* the worst schema in the batch and *structurally* one of the best, which is why the fix is targeted rather than a rewrite: the interface organisation, typed ids (`T.AudioStreamId`, `T.AudioFocusId`, `T.AudioDeviceId`), `T.Duration`, `T.Pagination` and `cancel` on every subscription are all correct, and `HrtfData.impulseData` (audio.capnp:817) is a model `T.DataHandle` bulk payload. But it contains **the batch's worst zero-copy trap** — `AudioDriverStream.submit @0 (frames :Data)` at audio.capnp:892, the actual driver boundary, where every PCM frame is copied into an RPC message — plus an `AudioTransport` enum that hard-codes `pulseAudio`, `alsa` and `pipewire` (audio.capnp:768-771), a 32-value `CodecFamily` enum naming eleven vendors, a `StreamRole.duckable` value that duplicates a `FocusRequest.duckable` field, two parallel channel-layout vocabularies plus four `Text` layout fields, the ALSA nouns `card`/`profile` on `AudioDevice`, a `MicPolicy` app allow/deny list, metering that can only be obtained by polling, and two unreachable driver interfaces. DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md:454 uses audio.capnp as its PipeWire swap-test example ("PipeWire → custom Rust audio server ✅ Yes"); that claim fails on the current file.

## Wire-breakers
- **No compile errors, no ordinal collisions** (verified mechanically).
- `audio.capnp:892` — `AudioDriverStream.submit @0 (frames :Data)` is the only frame-carrying method in the file. `AudioDriverStream` (audio.capnp:888-899) is **returned by no method** (see R8), so the copy-forcing path is also unreachable — but it is the declared driver boundary, so a 2026 driver implementer reading it will copy PCM per submit.
- `audio.capnp:840-843` — `AudioProvider.openStream @1 (config, direction) -> (stream :AudioStream)` returns a 20-field `AudioStream` struct, while `closeStream @2 (streamId :UInt64)` takes a bare `UInt64`. The file's own header says all UInt64 stream ids were replaced by `T.AudioStreamId` (audio.capnp:10) — this one was missed. A caller holding an `AudioStream` cannot tell whether to pass `stream.id.id` or something else, and nothing states that the provider's id space and the service's id space are the same.

## Duplicates & overlaps
- `AudioSampleFormat`/`AudioFormat` ≡ `T.PcmFormat` (types.capnp:1732-1749) — two models of PCM in the protocol, disagreeing on `isUnsigned` and on endianness handling.
- `ChannelMode` vs `ChannelLayout` vs 4 `Text` fields (above) — six representations of channel configuration.
- `ThermalState` here (audio.capnp:1194 is `HealthStatus`; the `ThermalState` duplicate is device.capnp:368 ≡ power.capnp:171) — cited in those files.
- `AudioSession`/`AudioSessionRequest`/`CrossDeviceRouting` (audio.capnp:721-747) duplicate session.capnp's session model and device.capnp's handoff concept; `syncPlayback`/`latencyMode` belong to no other audio concept.
- `HrtfData` (audio.capnp:813-824) vs `SpatialProfile.hrtf :Text` (audio.capnp:418) — HRTF is both a free-text name and a structured payload, with no rule.
- `micAccess`/`MicPolicy` (audio.capnp:983-1048) vs capture.capnp's `CapturePermission`/`CameraService` (capture.capnp:352-365) — two consent models, one for audio and one for screen, neither expressed as capabilities.
- `getGlobalDuckPolicy`/`setGlobalDuckPolicy` (audio.capnp:102-103) vs `duckOnCall @34` (per-stream `DuckPolicy`) vs `setPerRoleDuckLevels @56` — **three ducking mechanisms** with no precedence rule.

## Gaps & holes
- **No backpressure on the stream path.** `ArtifactStream` in ai.capnp:274-279 solves this with `pull(maxBytes)`. Here the only delivery primitive is `submit(frames :Data)` with no length, no frame count, and no completion notification beyond `VoidResult`, and the doc comment says "Framing is implementation-defined; consumer uses the negotiated sampleFormat" (audio.capnp:890-891) — but `openStream` returns no negotiation outcome, so **the frame boundary and sample format are never agreed**. A 2026 driver and a 2026 driver cannot interoperate; each pair must be tested. High fork risk, concrete and cheap to fix (return `T.PcmFormat` from `openStream`).
- **Metering/analysis can only be polled.** `getStreamMetering @46`, `getDeviceMetering @47`, `getStreamAnalysis @67`, `getDeviceAnalysis @68` are snapshot getters; `subscribeAnalysis @69` exists but only for *streams*. There is no metering subscription and no device-analysis subscription. A VU meter or an accessibility level indicator must poll at 30-60 Hz forever, waking the audio service and the client each time — on a phone that is showing a level meter, that is a continuous battery cost.
- **No underrun/overrun recovery contract.** `AudioStream.underrunCount`/`overrunCount` (audio.capnp:290-292) counters exist, but no event, no threshold, and no method to react, so a client cannot tell a glitching stream from a healthy one without polling the counters.
- **No "device gone" semantics.** `DeviceHealthEvent` carries `unplugged @3 :T.AudioDeviceId` (audio.capnp:1229) as an id with no last-known state, and nothing states what happens to streams routed to a device that disappears (re-route? fail? suspend?).
- **Consent has no escalation path.** `requestMicAccess @73` returns a decision, but nothing states how a later *new* source (e.g. the user plugs in a USB mic) re-opens consent, or how a consent revocation interacts with an in-flight stream.

## Zero-cost / eco
- **`submit (frames :Data)` (audio.capnp:892)** — the batch's worst zero-copy trap; see R2 above. Fix: `frames :T.DataHandle`, whose `inline` variant keeps small frames cheap and whose `buffer` variant removes the copy. Net: no added machinery, strictly less copying.
- **Polling-forcing metering** (`getStreamMetering`/`getDeviceMetering`, audio.capnp:46-47, 67-68) — 30-60 wakeups/s for a level display; a pull-based subscription with `cancel` (the schema's own canonical pattern) removes it.
- **`getBattery`-style telemetry**: not present here (power.capnp has it), but `subscribeAnalysis @69` has no minimum-interval or coalescing rule, so a 100 Hz analysis stream wakes the client 100 times a second.
- **Correct usage to preserve:** `HrtfData.impulseData :T.DataHandle` (audio.capnp:817) is the only bulk payload done right in this file — an HRTF impulse response is tens of KB and correctly a buffer reference.
- `AudioMetering.channelPeaks :List(Float32)` (audio.capnp:601) and `AudioAnalysis.peakLevels/rmsLevels :List(Float32)` (954-956) are bounded by channel count (~2-64 floats). **Not** R2 violations; flagging them would be noise.
- Unbounded inline lists: `LoudnessHistory.samples :List(LoudnessSample)` (audio.capnp:1076) with `getLoudnessHistory @80 (duration :T.Duration)` — a client may request a whole day of exposure samples inline, and there is no pagination on it.

## UX & implementer guidance
- `AudioDevice.id` is already `T.AudioDeviceId` (audio.capnp:235) — correct and opaque. **DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md:165, :464 and :530 are stale on this point**, claiming `AudioDevice.id` is `Text` and needs fixing; the fix has already landed, and the doctrine's verdict table still lists audio.capnp as "clean" while it has 21 enums and the batch's worst R2 trap.
- `setStreamVolume @5`/`getStreamMetering @46` take `T.AudioStreamId`, but `AudioProvider.closeStream @2` takes `UInt64` — two stream-id spellings in one file (audio.capnp:42 vs 843).
- `applyPreset @41 (presetId :Text)` and `saveCustomPreset @42 → (presetId :Text)` use `Text` ids while `AudioPreset.id @0 :Text` — consistent, but the id space is caller-chosen with no uniqueness rule, so two apps can collide on `"flat"`.
- `negotiateCodec @29 (streamId, codec :T.CodecId)` negotiates a *typed* codec, but `AudioCodec.family :CodecFamily` (audio.capnp:610) is an enum — so a codec can be addressed by opaque id but described by a frozen vendor list.
- `SoundEvent.eventId :Text` with the example `"click", "alert.warning", "notification.new"` (audio.capnp:910) — an undefined dotted vocabulary; nothing registers which ids exist except `listSoundEvents @64`, which is paginated but has no filter.
- `declareRecordingPolicy @27 (policy :RecordingPolicy)` sets a per-app recording policy with no parameter saying *which* app (the `appId` is inside the struct, so a client can set another app's policy).

## Recommended actions
1. **`AudioDriverStream.submit`: `frames :Data` → `frames :T.DataHandle`.** Gain: removes the copy on the hottest path in the batch (192 KB/s per stream; 100 allocations/s per stream); aligns with ai.capnap:74-75 and the file's own `HrtfData` precedent. Cost: driver resolves a buffer handle — one indirection, and `DataHandle.inline` still covers sub-1 KB frames.
2. **Return the negotiated `T.PcmFormat` from `AudioProvider.openStream`.** Gain: frame boundary and sample format become an agreed contract instead of "implementation-defined" (audio.capnp:890-891), so two independently-written drivers interoperate. Cost: one result field.
3. **Delete `AudioTransport` or reduce it to an open `Text` token; delete `CodecFamily`/`AudioContainerFormat` in favour of `T.MimeType`.** Gain: removes the Linux server names (`alsa`, `pulseAudio`, `pipewire`) that break the doctrine's own swap test, and stops freezing 32 codec names into an eternal schema. Cost: consumers lose typed codec comparison — which they only had for today's codecs anyway; `T.MimeType` + `metadata` covers it (ai.capnp:142-155).
4. **Unify the channel-layout representations**: pick `ChannelLayout` (the richer, spatial one), type the four `Text` fields with it, and delete `ChannelMode`'s spatial members (`mono/stereo/surround51/surround71/ambisonic`) leaving only the *strategy* (`automatic/forced/binaural`). Gain: one spelling, type-safe layouts, and a 2046 7.1.4/ambisonic layout becomes expressible. Cost: four field type changes.
5. **Remove `StreamRole.duckable @11` and `emergency @12`; keep `FocusRequest.duckable` and a separate `StreamFlags`.** Gain: one authority path for the duck decision, exactly as ai.capnp:53 and DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md:394 require. Cost: two enum values removed.
6. **Add metering/analysis subscriptions** (`MeteringSubscription`, `DeviceAnalysisSubscription`) with pull + cancel, matching the schema's own pattern. Gain: removes the 30-60 Hz polling battery drain for every level meter and accessibility indicator. Cost: ~12 lines.
7. **Gate the privileged surface behind capability accessors** on `interface Audio` (ai.capnp:578-584): `micControl`, `routing`, `settings`, `safety` each taking a `Capability`; replace `MicPolicy.blockedApps/trustedApps` with capability targets. Gain: a sandboxed app can no longer revoke another app's mic consent or disable hearing protection; removes the forbidden permission lists. Cost: an accessor layer and a migration of 5 methods.
8. **Fix `closeStream @2`** to `T.AudioStreamId`, and make `getStreamAnalysis`/`getDeviceAnalysis` consistent (both subscrippable). Gain: removes the UInt64 and the polling asymmetry. Cost: one field, two methods.
9. Leave `LatencyClass`, `SoundCategory`, `MicSourceType`, `AudioEffectType` and `PresetCategory` alone: each has a `custom` escape and low stakes; converting them to open tokens costs consumers a filtering vocabulary and buys little.
