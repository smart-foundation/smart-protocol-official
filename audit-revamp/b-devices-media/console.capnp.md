# audit: `schema/console.capnp` (723 lines)

## Verdict
**FIX (targeted) — the schema whose header contradicts its own body most sharply.** The header claims "Zero-backend-assumption IPC contract for terminal interaction. No termcap, ncurses, xterm, VT100, or platform-specific terminal emulator assumed" (console.capnp:6-9), and for the Audit-7 half that is true: `ConsoleMode {terminal, voice, braille, spatial, haptic, instrumentCluster}` (155-163) and the `OutputCapability`/`InputCapability` lists (166-189) are a genuinely timeless bet that a 2046 voice-only, braille, or AR console is expressible today. But the v0.0.2 half (console.capnp:600-723) hard-codes the **Linux kernel's tty architecture**: `TtyClass {virtualConsole ("vc0..vcN"), graphicalTerminal, serialConsole, kernelLogConsole, bootSplash, safeModeShell, remoteConsole ("SSH, IPMI, ...")}`, and `ConsoleSession.label` is documented with the literal Linux device-node names `"vc0", "ttyS0", "kmsg"` (640-642). That is a direct R5 failure, in the section that is least timeless. Alongside it: `ConsoleSession.permissions :UInt32` with a `ConsolePermission` enum that has **no bit assignment** (a required bitmask with no defined bits), `playback` returning an unbounded `List(InputEvent)`, a `RecordingFormat` enum that replicates the exact `LogFormat` anti-pattern the doctrine flags as 🔴 High, `readInput @0` as a blocking read alongside `subscribeInput`, and an `interface ConsoleRecording` that duplicates the service's own eight recording methods and is reachable from nothing.

## Wire-breakers
- **None.** No compile errors, no ordinal collisions (verified mechanically).
- `console.capnp:654-667` — `enum ConsolePermission {inputReadable, inputWritable, outputReadable, outputWritable, forceCloseable, custom}` while `ConsoleSession.permissions @7 :UInt32` (651). **The enum declares the vocabulary but the wire field is a raw bitmask and no bit assignment exists anywhere.** An implementer cannot know whether `inputReadable` is bit 0 or bit 1, so two backends' `permissions` values are mutually unreadable. This is the same defect as usb.capnp's `workaroundFlags :UInt32`, but here the enum that *should* define the bits is right there in the file and does not.
- `console.capnp:406, 546` — `playback @25 (sessionId, fromTime :T.Timestamp) -> (events :List(InputEvent))` (and its duplicate `ConsoleRecording.playback @4`) is **unbounded**: a recorded session is hours of input, and there is no pagination and no stream. A 2046 replay of a long session ships all events in one message.
- `console.capnp:243` is display.capnp's `writeConsoleText → ()`; console.capnp's own `writeText @2 (text :Text) -> T.VoidResult` (359) is correctly typed but accepts **unbounded text** with no framing or backpressure.

## Duplicates & overlaps
- `ConsoleService`'s 8 recording methods ≡ `ConsoleRecording`'s 8 methods (same file, same ordinals).
- `ConsoleSandbox` vs sandbox.capnp; `ThemePreference` vs theme.capnp/settings.capnp; `setLanguage`/`getLanguage` vs translation.capnp/settings.capnp; `ConsolePowerMode` vs `T.PowerSleepState`/power.capnp/device.capnp; `AccessibilityRole` vs accessibility.capnp; `SubtitleEntry` vs accessibility.capnp/translation.capnp.
- `KeyEvent.key @0 :Text` (console.capnp:49) vs input.capnp's `T.KeyCode.code :UInt32` (types.capnp:928) — **two key-identity representations in the batch, neither with a defined spelling** (see the input audit; this is the batch's worst implementer trap, appearing in both schemas).
- `InputEvent` (console.capnp:30-46) vs input.capnp's `InputEvent` (692-701) — **same struct name, different content** (console's carries key/mouse/resize/paste/stylus/eye/spatial; input's carries eye/brain/spatial/crown/cabin). A consumer importing both gets an identifier clash.
- `StylusInputEvent` (console.capnp:258-269) vs input.capnp's `TouchPoint`/stylus and display.capnp's `StylusDisplayEvent` — a third stylus representation.
- `AudioOutputEvent.text @0 :Text` + `language :T.Language` + `rate :Float32` (192-201) vs speech.capnp — speech synthesis requested by a console with no speech-schema involvement.

## Gaps & holes
- **No output-flow control.** `writeText @2`/`writeStyled @3` have no backpressure, no "are you full" signal, and no way for a consumer to stop a runaway producer. Every pager and `yes`-style program relies on terminal flow control that the protocol does not model.
- **No scrollback/history contract.** `TerminalInfo` has no scrollback capacity, and there is no way to read history — so a console emulator cannot implement scrollback portably.
- **No coordinate space for `OutputZone`/`InstrumentClusterZone`** — `InstrumentClusterZone.id @0 :UInt32` and `OutputZone.id @0 :UInt32` are independent id spaces (`setOutputZone @20 (zone :OutputZone)` vs `writeGauge @16 (zoneId :UInt32, value :Text)`), with `writeGauge`'s value a bare `Text` (formatted number? unit?).
- **No multi-session focus arbitration.** `ConsoleMultiplexer.setFocus @5 (handle :UInt64)`/`getFocus @6` take raw `UInt64` handles while `ConsoleSession.handle @0 :UInt64` — untagged, so a `UInt64` from any other handle space (window id, macro id, job id) is accepted.
- **No resize/geometry negotiation**: `ConsoleWindowConfig.size :T.Vec2i` and `ResizeEvent {columns, rows}` are unrelated (pixels vs cells), and nothing states which is authoritative after a resize.
- **No keyboard-focus vs pointer-focus distinction** (`FocusChangeEvent.focused @0 :Bool` is a single boolean), so a console cannot distinguish an input-method grab from a visual focus.
- **No recovery contract beyond `recoverSession @35 → ConsoleRecoveryInfo`** (pending counts only, no data, no replay).

## Zero-cost / eco
- **`playback @25`/`@4` return an unbounded `List(InputEvent)`** (console.capnp:406, 546) — a whole session's events in one message, both on the reachable method and on the duplicate interface. Fix: a pull-based stream with a max count, or a `T.DataHandle`.
- **`writeText @2 (text :Text)`** accepts unbounded text (console.capnp:359) — a `cat` of a large file copies the whole thing per call, and the receiver must allocate for it.
- **`writeStyled @3 (styled :StyledText)`** ships `segments :List(TextSegment)` where every segment carries a full `TextStyle` with two `T.Color`s (console.capnp:97-116). A full-screen styled repaint (an editor at 2000 cells × ~24 bytes of style) is tens of KB copied per frame, with no partial/damage update — the "no runtime diffing / explicit mutations only" principle (TIMELESSNESS.md:128) is satisfied, but at the cost of full-segment restyling.
- **`InputSubscription.pull @0 () -> (events :List(InputEvent), total :UInt32)`** (console.capnp:495) returns a *batch* of key/mouse events unbounded — no limit, no coalescing, so an unpulled subscription grows without bound server-side while a slow consumer's next pull ships everything at once.
- **Idle-power:** `getInfo @7`, `getCapabilities @9`, `getLanguage @18`, `getThemePreference @43`, `getCursor @8` are poll-only getters; `subscribeCursor @10` exists but `getCursor` has no change event, so a cursor-position consumer polls at frame rate.
- No bulk pixel/sample payloads in this file — it is a control/text schema. `exportRecording @26 → T.DataHandle` (407) is correct for a whole recording.

## UX / implementer guidance
- `writeGauge @16 (zoneId :UInt32, value :Text)` — the gauge value is a `Text` with no format, so a backend must parse a string to set a numeric value; the zone id is a separate `UInt32` space from `OutputZone.id`.
- `negotiateFormat @34` returns a single `OutputCapability`, so a backend that supports both `styledText` and `audio` can express only one — a 2046 mixed-mode console must pick.
- `clear @6 (area :ClearArea)` mixes line-relative and screen-relative clears in one enum with no cursor-dependence statement.
- `KeyEvent.rawByte @1 :UInt8` (console.capnp:50) alongside `key :Text` — an undefined 8-bit code with no stated meaning (a scancode? a byte of a multibyte sequence?).
- `InputEvent`'s union ordinals skip @0 (fields start at @1 with `timestamp @0` outside the union) — harmless, but a reader may assume `@0` is a valid discriminant.
- `MouseEvent.delta @5 :T.Vec2i` is documented as "for relative motion reporting" while `position @0 :T.Vec2i` is absolute; nothing states whether a backend reports one or both, and a client cannot tell which is present (both are always present as structs).

## Recommended actions
1. **Replace `permissions :UInt32` with `List(ConsolePermission)`** (or define the bit assignment explicitly). Gain: a required bitmask becomes readable across backends and extensible; the enum already exists and already lists the values. Cost: one field type — the cheapest high-value fix in the file.
2. **Purge the v0.0.2 Linux console architecture**: replace `TtyClass` with transport-neutral roles (an interactive console, a log sink, a boot surface, an emergency shell, a remote-attached console) and delete the `"vc0"/"ttyS0"/"kmsg"` examples and the SSH/IPMI mentions from required positions and docs. Gain: the file's own header claim becomes true; a 2046 spatial/voice/attached console needs no fiction. Cost: one enum rewritten + doc cleanup.
3. **Fix `RecordingFormat`** → `T.MimeType` or an open `Text` token (the doctrine's exact prescription for the `LogFormat` anti-pattern, DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md:226). Gain: a 2046 tool can request CBOR/protobuf; `exportRecording` stops being closed by today's formats. Cost: one type change.
4. **Choose one input pattern**: delete `readInput @0` (blocking, uncancellable, no error) and keep `subscribeInput`, or make `readInput` a bounded `pull (maxCount)`. Gain: removes the dual-pattern problem and the infinite-block trap on a voice-only console. Cost: one method deleted or changed.
5. **Bound `playback` and `InputSubscription.pull`** (a max count, or a `T.DataHandle`), and add backpressure/chunking to `writeText`/`writeStyled`. Gain: a long replay and a runaway `cat` stop materialising whole messages. Cost: parameters.
6. **Delete `interface ConsoleRecording`** (its eight methods are duplicates of `ConsoleService`'s eight) or make it reachable. Gain: one authority path for recording. Cost: a deletion.
7. **Add `unknown @N :UnknownEvent {typeId, payload}` to `ConsoleMuxEvent` and `InputEvent.custom`** (currently `Void`/`Data`). Gain: forward-compatible events in the doctrine's exact shape. Cost: two variants.
8. **Cut the cross-domain policy blocks**: `ConsolePowerMode`, `ThemePreference` (+4 methods), `setLanguage`/`getLanguage`, `ConsoleSandbox` (+3 methods), `SubtitleEntry`/`writeSubtitle`, `AccessibilityRole`/`setAccessibilityMetadata`. Gain: a terminal-I/O schema stops duplicating power/theme/settings/sandbox/accessibility/translation schemas, and five vocabularies collapse to their canonical homes. Cost: those concerns move to their owning schemas — which is where they already are.
9. Leave `ConsoleMode`, `OutputCapability`, `InputCapability`, the braille/haptic/spatial output events, `CursorStyle`, `ClearArea` and `TerminalCapabilities` alone: the Audit-7 half of this file is the most future-looking design in the batch and should be preserved verbatim.
