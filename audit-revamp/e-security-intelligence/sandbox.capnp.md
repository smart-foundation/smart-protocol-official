# Audit — sandbox.capnp (396 lines)

## Verdict
**FIX (targeted).** The file's subtitle is "process sandboxing, capability control, isolation boundaries" and its header claims "Zero backend assumptions: no seccomp, no landlock, no namespace, no WASI, no gVisor, no Firecracker" (`:8-10`). The body contradicts that claim in at least six places: seccomp's filter-action names, Linux mount flags, and RLIMIT field names are all present in required positions. The capability-control half is worse — the subtitle says "capability control" and the file implements a permission-string model that `DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md:399` explicitly forbids. The zero-cost defect is the file's largest: a whole sandbox's memory snapshot returned as inline `Data`.

## Wire-breakers
None. Method ordinals @0–@18 unique (`:15-72`); struct ordinals collision-free; all `T.*` references resolve (`T.SandboxId`, `T.ProcessId`, `T.SignalKind`, `T.ExitStatus`, `T.ResourceUsage`, `T.IsolatedScope` all defined).

## Duplicates & overlaps
- `CapabilityGrant`/rights/resource ↔ `capability.capnp:22-53` (the canonical model) ↔ `content.capnp:173-200` `AccessRule` ↔ `runtime_search.capnp:250-257` `CapabilityPermission` ↔ `auth.capnp:196-217` `PermissionGrant` — five permission models in one batch.
- `ResourceLimits` (`:288-298`) ↔ `types.capnp:1776-1783` `Quota` (memory/cpu/io/pids/wallClock) — two resource-limit structs with different field names and no declared relationship, plus `types.capnp:892-901` `T.ResourceUsage` (used correctly here at `:38`).
- `SandboxPolicy.filesystemAccess/networkAccess/ipcAccess` ↔ `files.capnp`'s permission model and `network.capnp`'s rules — sandbox is a third place access policy is expressed.
- `PolicyViolation` (`:350-355` here) ↔ `access.capnp:184-193` `PolicyViolation` (**same name, different fields**: `policyType`/`rule`/`action`/`denied` vs `policy`/`reason`/`requirement`/`overrideable`). A file importing both has two types by one name.
- `ResourceType` (`:373-382`) mirrors `T.ResourceUsage`'s fields as an enum; `PolicyType` (`:357-365`) mirrors the policy struct's own subsections.

## Gaps & holes
- **No contract for what a restored sandbox's capabilities are.** `restore @16 (state :Data) -> (result :CreateSandboxResult)` (`:65`) returns only ids; the restored sandbox's `CapabilityGrant` set, its `SandboxPolicy`, and its granted `Capability`s are all undefined. Demonstrated fork risk: one backend restores capabilities from the snapshot, another requires re-granting, and a consumer cannot know which it got.
- `configureFilter @1 (sandboxId, filter)` (`:20`) and `updatePolicy @3 (sandboxId, policy)` (`:26`) are live policy updates with no contract for in-flight operations: does a process mid-syscall get the old rule or the new one? Every backend decides.
- `freeze @13`/`thaw @14` (`:56`, `:59`) have no declared relationship to `terminateSandbox @6 (signal :T.SignalKind)` (`:35`) or to the resource accounting that continues while frozen.
- `checkBackend @9 (backend :SandboxBackend) -> (available :Bool, version :Text)` (`:44`) returns a `version :Text` for a backend "kind" (`wasm`/`native`/`hardware`, `:315-320`) with no declared version scheme — an implementer must guess what to put there.

## Zero-cost & eco
One severe defect (`save`/`restore`, see R2) and otherwise a clean profile: `CreateSandboxRequest` limits and metadata are small, and `getResourceUsage @7` returns `T.ResourceUsage` (bounded, typed). `listSandboxes @4` is paginated (`:29`). `SandboxSubscription.pull @0` (`:75`) is unbounded but policy events are naturally low-volume.

## UX & implementer guidance
- `SandboxBackend` (`:315-320`) names `native`, which is the least meaningful word available: two `native` backends may share no mechanism, and a consumer selecting `native` gets an implementation-defined sandbox.
- `SimulationResult.violations @1 :List(Text)` and `EvaluationResult.recommendations @2 :List(Text)` (`:386-396`) are free-form advice strings — an implementer must invent their vocabulary and no consumer can act on them.
- `evaluate @18 (policy :SandboxPolicy) -> (result :EvaluationResult)` (`:71`) takes no `sandboxId`, so it evaluates a policy against nothing — what does it mean to evaluate a policy with no sandbox attached? The contract is unclear by construction.
- `ResourceExhausted.resource @0 :ResourceType` (`:368`) correctly names which limit was hit with `current`/`limit` — the honest event shape the file should use more.
- The header's "Used by: process.capnp (sandboxed spawning), init.capnp (service isolation)" (`:12`) is accurate; the types (`T.SandboxId`) were promoted to `types.capnp:1128` with a cross-schema note that is honoured.

## Recommended actions
1. **Delete the seccomp/mount/rlimit vocabulary: replace `FilterAction`'s mechanism names with allow/deny (plus `custom` for backend-specific actions), make `MountRule`'s flags an open `options :T.StringMap` (keeping `readonly`, which is universal), and rename `ResourceLimits`' rlimit fields to backend-neutral names.** Gain: non-Linux backends stop having to fake POSIX semantics in required fields, and the header's "zero backend assumptions" claim becomes true. Cost: consumers reading `errno`/`nosuid`/`maxOpenFiles` must read open options — and there are no consumers, since nothing external consumes the protocol.
2. **Replace `CapabilityGrant` with a `Capability` reference (or a target+operations pair) and delete the sandbox-local grant/revoke lifecycle in favour of `capability.capnp`'s `attenuate`/`revoke`/`revokeControlled`.** Gain: the file's advertised "capability control" becomes real, and two revocation paths become one. Cost: `grantCapability`/`revokeCapability`/`listCapabilities` change shape.
3. **Return `T.DataHandle` from `save` and accept it in `restore`.** Gain: sandbox migration stops being a copy of possibly gigabytes through the message arena; cost: one field type each, and callers must allocate a handle — exactly what the type exists for.
4. **Define the capability state of a restored sandbox.** Gain: a fork closes (restore-revokes vs restore-preserves); cost: one comment plus, ideally, a `capabilities` field on `CreateSandboxResult`.
5. **Type `IpcAccess.allowedServices` as `T.ServiceId`.** Gain: IPC allow-lists become correlatable with actual service references; cost: a field type.
6. **Merge or rename `PolicyViolation` and `ResourceLimits` against their same-named counterparts** (`access.capnp:184`, `types.capnp:1776`). Gain: one name per concept; cost: a rename in one file.
